Aloft

You are a young crane. The flock left while you were sleeping. It is a long way south, and you are one day behind.

Living design doc · v1.0 · Nov 24 2026 · supersedes v0.10 (kept in docs/archive/) · updated in place when decisions land

The color script, start to finish. Position in it follows distance flown, not a clock.

What the first version was

The July prototype asked one question at a time and answered four of them: gliding feels good, finding lift is satisfying, the air can be read without a HUD, and a bird with soft music has something. The heart of it was right and is untouched here: height and speed are the same thing in two forms, and only the world can give you more. The flight constants you tuned are the same numbers.

The honest verdict at gate 3 was "heart-adjacent", and the note from the last playtests was "kind of empty". I think both had one cause. It was a sandbox with a wonderful verb and no reason to use it: nowhere you needed to be, no one who'd notice, and a look that showed you the air without making you want to be in it. Adding biomes and journals to that would have decorated the emptiness. So instead of continuing phase 4, this is a retelling.

The retelling

Aloft is now a migration. Six lands, one flight each, from the marsh where you hatched to the water where everyone winters. The flock is always ahead. You can't see it, but you can call, and it answers.

That call is the whole design in one button. It tells you where to go (the answer comes from somewhere), it shows you the air (your voice lights up every thermal and wind stroke around you for a few seconds), and it is the story: on day one the reply is a few faint voices over the horizon, and each day it is nearer and larger, until the last morning it is thousands. Your own voice grows up along the way. Day one is a thin juvenile trill. By the end it's a bugle.

The theme is lateness, and being forgiven for it. The sun doesn't move unless you do: the day waits for you. Landing anywhere is rest, not a reset. The last thing that happens in the game is that you hear someone further behind than you ever were, and you are the one who answers.

Pillars

  • Calm, gentle, zen still outranks everything. Nothing snaps. Nothing fails. When two tunings both work, ship the gentler.
  • The glide is sacred. The tuned energy model is the foundation. New verbs sit beside it and may not create energy from the stick.
  • The world teaches the wind. Helices and motes in thermals, cumulus over them, cranes already circling, grass and treetops bending in the same gust, cloths on the standing stones, cat's-paws on water, glow in the night sea.
  • You are not alone for long. Solitary at the start, accompanied by the end.
  • Every frame a postcard. Photo mode literally makes one, with the place name on it.

The verbs

Glide

Unchanged. Pitch trades height for speed and back; bank turns; hands off settles to a glide. One kindness added: rising air shelters you from wind drift (liftHold), so circling is lean-and-rest, not constant correction.

Breath (hold Space)

Wingbeats push and lift, and spend breath. Rising air, resting and the flock's dropped feathers give it back. This is the old "boost meter, charged by riding lift" idea made physical: energy you found, banked and released. About nine seconds in a full breath: enough to leave a shore and reach the first lift, not enough to ignore it. The meter is a feather.

Call (E)

Wayfinding, air-reading and story, as above. Also how you greet birds you meet.

Company

Other stragglers wait in thermals and beside landmarks: two each in the first three lands, one each in the next two. They join for the rest of the journey, fly ahead of you in a wide open V (you're the youngest, so you fly at the back), save you a little drag, and each adds a voice to the music.

Feathers

The flock sheds feathers along good lines: spirals up thermals, skims over water, under the Nine Arches. They're the lesson and the line at once. Each plays the next note of the home motif, so flying a line well plays its melody.

Rest

Touch down anywhere, including shallow water, and the bird stands. Breath comes back. Space runs, leaps and flies. Deep water can't be stood in, so it lifts you off its back. Steep ground turns you along itself. There is no crash, reset or respawn in the game.

Drift (Q)

The bird flies itself: works thermals, keeps the road, comes down at the roost. The game can be played with one hand, or none.

Each land is 16 km across and about 11 km along its road: 8 to 12 minutes flown straight, longer if you wander. Every land stays open from The road once reached. The July decision that a seed is a map survives there: type a number, or deal another, and that land's country is rebuilt from it, always the same.

How a day goes

Wake where you slept. Leap. The light advances with distance flown. At dusk the whole land breathes out a little lift (the glass-off), so no one is stranded at the end. The roost brings you down itself. Companions land around you, two lines of verse, a small log, stars. The next land bakes while you sleep, so there are no loading screens after the first.

Art direction

  • A painted sky with physical manners. Each land is five or six hand-mixed palettes; everything in frame (terrain, feather, leaf, water, cloud, fog) reads light and air from the same functions, so the frame always belongs to one moment.
  • Light does the emotional work. Volumetric clouds and valley mist with real shafts, mountain shadows kilometres long, sun through leaves and flight feathers, a road of light on the water.
  • Brushwork. Surfaces are painted with a generated dab texture; a light painterly filter (adjustable in Settings) settles color into strokes.
  • The crane is the only fine detail. About 340 feathers over a posed skeleton, no animation clips, so it never loops. It starts rust-brown like a real juvenile and whitens across the six days.
  • The July rule "flat-shaded low-poly" is retired. It was a cost decision. "Atmosphere over detail" was the real rule, and it stays.

Sound

All synthesised, nothing sampled. Tones over texture stays law: the first version's wind read as static on laptop speakers. Slow pads, a sparse melody whose spacing tightens with airspeed, a musical variometer that brightens as you climb, one instrument color per land, a voice per companion. The feather motif recurs in every land's mode, hangs on its last note in five of them, and resolves only in the last. Levels, peaks and spectra were measured offline (every land within a decibel, no clicks, almost nothing above 6 kHz); no person has listened to it yet, so balance and reverb are the first things to check by ear.

Detail calls made, open to veto

  • The premise: lateness and migration, a crane, the six lands and their names, every line of verse.
  • Light follows distance, never time.
  • Companions lead and you fly at the back, rather than you at the point.
  • Auto-landing inside 300 m of the roost.
  • Eight companions at most. They can't be lost.
  • No map, compass or objective marker: the call is the only wayfinding.
  • Units are metres and km/h, as before.
  • The Bell That Waits sits inside the cloud deck; you find it by feel.

Architecture

  • src/sim/ pure logic, no Three.js, no DOM. Flight model, air, flock, autopilot. npm run test:sim.
  • src/world/ pure and worker-safe: land recipes, hydraulic erosion, baking. npm run test:journey flies every land headlessly end to end.
  • src/render/ a custom HDR pipeline on three.js WebGL2: CDLOD terrain, deferred air, raymarched clouds with temporal accumulation, baked terrain shadows plus one near cascade, screen-space water reflection, instanced forests, AgX grade.
  • src/game/ the movements (all art direction is data in days.ts), flow, save.
  • src/audio/, src/input/, src/ui/.
  • Four quality tiers, picked automatically, plus dynamic resolution underneath.
  • No downloaded assets: all geometry, textures and sound are generated. The whole game is one ~1 MB file.

What I'd look at next

  1. Play it on real GPUs. It was built and checked on a software renderer, so the pictures are verified and the frame rate is not.
  2. Flight pacing by hand. The autopilot finishes every land; a person should confirm the first thermal is found without frustration.
  3. A Jess pass on palettes, if she'd enjoy it. Everything is hex values in days.ts.
  4. Parked: choosing your bird, real-elevation lands, a second journey north in spring.
